# Research Question Module

## Purpose

Complete the question-forming part of `research_focus`: convert a direction into an answerable core question, an explicit goal or deliberate deferral, and a defensible conclusion boundary.

## Required Inputs

- Selected topic.
- Confirmed real problem.
- Target object and scene.
- Initial method or intervention direction.
- Evaluation angle or expected improvement.
- Current goal, or an explicit decision to keep it `undetermined`.

## Coaching Loop

1. Explain the difference between topic, problem, task, outcome, and research question.
2. Route or deliberately defer the goal when the requested output depends on it.
3. Draft one core question.
4. Draft no more than three subquestions unless the teacher asks for a fuller framework.
5. State the conclusion boundary: what this design may show and what it cannot claim.
6. Explain whether subquestions are parallel or progressive.
7. Ask the teacher to select, rewrite, merge, or delete. A generic continuation does not answer a missing object, indicator, comparison, or feasibility fact.

## Pass Conditions

- The core question has object, scene, intended change or relationship, observable result, and evaluation angle.
- The teacher owns the core question through a direct selection, rewrite, or correction.
- The goal is explicit or deliberately `undetermined`, and no goal-specific output is silently assumed.
- The conclusion boundary is stated.

## Output

```markdown
### Research Questions

Core question:

Subquestions:
1. 
2. 
3. 

Relationship:

Current goal:

Conclusion boundary:

Revision notes:
```

## Fallback

If the teacher provides only a title, ask what exact change the project wants to produce and how the teacher would know that change happened.
